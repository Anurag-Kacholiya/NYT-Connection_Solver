## Analysis: Why This Method Doesn't Work Well

Even with the implementation of **Size-Constrained Clustering** (ensuring exactly 4 words per group), the **GloVe + K-Means Baseline** still struggles, achieving only 1 perfect solve out of 100 random games. While the structural constraint is now satisfied, several fundamental weaknesses remain:

1.  **Static Embeddings Lack Context (Polysemy)**
    GloVe assigns a *single, static* 300-dimensional vector to each word. In NYT Connections, words often have multiple meanings (polysemy), and their specific meaning in a board depends entirely on the other 15 words. A static embedding averages these meanings, creating semantic ambiguity that K-Means cannot resolve.

2.  **Distance Metric Mismatch**
    The game often uses abstract, phonetic, or structural categories (e.g., "Words that start with a body part" or "Palindromes") that do not map to semantic similarity in a vector space. K-Means seeks semantic proximity, but Connections categories are often cryptic and non-semantic.

3.  **Inability to Navigate "Red Herrings"**
    The puzzle is designed with "one-away" traps (e.g., 5 words that fit a category). Even with size constraints, K-Means will greedily pick the 4 most similar words, potentially breaking the global solution for the remaining 12 words.

4.  **Linear Assignment Optimization**
    While we now use the **Hungarian Algorithm (Linear Sum Assignment)** to enforce 4-word groups, this optimization still relies on the initial centroids found by K-Means. If the initial centroids are poorly placed due to the "Red Herring" effect or polysemy, the constrained assignment will simply be the "best of a bad set" of clusters.

## Hungarian Algorithm Explained
In our Connections solver, the **Hungarian Algorithm** (also known as the Linear Sum Assignment) is the "referee" that enforces the rules of the game.

- **The Problem**: Standard K-Means is "greedy." It might put 6 words in one group and 2 in another if they are semantically closer. This is illegal in Connections, where every group must have exactly 4 words.
- **The Solution**: We create 16 "slots" (4 seats for each of the 4 cluster centers).
- **The Optimization**: The algorithm looks at all possible ways to sit the 16 words into those 16 slots. It calculates a "cost" (distance) for every possible pairing.
- **The Result**: It finds the one specific configuration where every word is assigned to exactly one slot, and every cluster gets exactly 4 words, such that the total distance across all 16 words is as small as possible.

It essentially forces the model to find the "best-fitting" 4x4 balanced partition.
